### Choosing the segment and route for an inspection-systems maker entering North America

**Service:** GTM Setup · **Industry:** Industrial Technology · **Family:** GTM Strategy & Market Intelligence

*Every route into North American plants came with its own sponsor and numbers. Like-for-like evidence and written decision gates now decide which route is funded, and when.*

- **The challenge:** Several segments and routes into North America, each argued with incomparable evidence.
- **Our approach:** Like-for-like segment and route assessment, with gates naming the evidence each investment needs.
- **The result:** An entry plan that funds each route only when its gate evidence arrives.

| At a glance | |
|---|---|
| Client profile | European machine-vision and inspection systems manufacturer |
| Industry | Industrial Technology |
| Markets | United States and Mexico |
| Services | GTM Setup, Market Intelligence & Assessment, Channel Strategy |
| Engagement | Market-entry strategy and roadmap |

#### 01 · Situation

A European inspection-systems maker eyeing North America usually has more ways in than budget: direct to plant quality and operations heads, through integrators who build packaging and assembly lines, via industrial distributors, or embedded in machine builders' equipment. Each option arrives with its sponsor's own evidence, from a friendly machine builder's forecast to a distributor's catalogue reach, so none can be compared. Picking wrong costs a field team hired too early, or a design-in slot that stays closed until the machine builder's next platform.

#### 02 · Question

Which plant segment and route should the company enter through, and what evidence must each further investment wait for?

#### 03 · What LeadStrategus did

- **Build comparable evidence by segment.** Assess food and beverage packaging, Mexican automotive tier suppliers and pharmaceutical packaging on the same measures: plant concentration, inspection needs, incumbent vision vendors and who signs off.
- **Map the buyer in each segment.** Identify who decides and influences, from plant quality managers and controls engineers to integrator and machine-builder engineering heads, and what each needs before specifying a new vision vendor.
- **Compare routes on the same terms.** Score direct, integrator, distributor and OEM embed on time to revenue, cost to serve, support burden and customer control, using integrator and machine-builder interviews.
- **Write the decision gates.** For each investment step, state the evidence required, the owner and the spend released. Example: the direct field team is funded only after a reference plant runs in production and committed integrator partners have signed.

#### 04 · Evidence / deliverables

- Segment assessment on common measures
- Buyer map covering plants, integrators and machine builders
- Route comparison: direct, integrator, distributor and OEM embed
- Decision-gate register: evidence, owner and spend released at each gate
- Sequenced entry roadmap for the United States and Mexico

#### 05 · Outcome

- **Leadership** releases entry spend gate by gate, against evidence agreed in advance
- **Route sponsors** bring evidence in the agreed format, measured the same way
- **Direct hiring** held until a reference plant is running in production
- **OEM talks** limited to machine builders with a named platform launch

Entry discussions now open with the gate register: what evidence has arrived and which spend it releases.

#### 06 · What changed in the client's GTM motion

A quarterly gate review now releases or holds spend for each route. New options, such as a distributor approaching unprompted, are scored on the agreed measures before anyone debates them. Sales, marketing and partner plans follow the roadmap that review updates.

### Choosing the first US financial-services segment an IT services firm can win

**Service:** GTM Setup · **Industry:** IT Services · **Family:** GTM Strategy & Market Intelligence

*The firm had a micro-market map but no starting point. Segment choice, buyer personas and a gated entry motion turn the map into decisions.*

- **The challenge:** A micro-market map in hand, but no agreed first segment, buyer or motion.
- **Our approach:** Segment choice, buyer personas and a first motion, built on the existing map.
- **The result:** An entry segment chosen on evidence, and a gate before the next opens.

| At a glance | |
|---|---|
| Client profile | IT services firm expanding into the US |
| Industry | IT Services |
| Markets | United States, financial services |
| Services | GTM Setup, OSINT / Market & Account Intelligence, Market Intelligence & Assessment |
| Engagement | Market-entry plan |

#### 01 · Situation

An IT services firm entering US financial services often holds a micro-market map but no decision on where to start. Large banks buy through preferred-supplier panels where established integrators hold master agreements. Spreading a small team across banks, insurers and credit unions burns selling time before anyone learns who will engage. Every deal also meets a test the firm has not prepared for: a bank's vendor-risk review of offshore delivery.

#### 02 · Question

Which segment should the firm enter first, who decides there, and what will a bank's vendor-risk review demand?

#### 03 · What LeadStrategus did

- **Start from the existing micro-market map.** Score the evidence-backed segments from the earlier OSINT investigation on technology need, incumbent-integrator lock-in, deal size and the firm's proof points.
- **Choose the entry segment and record why.** Regional banks on ageing core platforms win: thin in-house engineering, modernisation work the firm already delivers and few master agreements with large integrators. Large banks are deferred; credit unions are parked because many buy through shared service organisations.
- **Build personas around real objections.** Profile the CIO, the head of digital banking and the third-party risk lead. For the risk lead, the objection is offshore delivery: who can see customer data, from where, and through which subcontractors.
- **Design the entry motion and its gate.** Set the target list, core message, channel mix and a vendor-risk pack sellers share before it is requested. Agree with leadership that the next segment opens only once the first yields referenceable clients.

#### 04 · Evidence / deliverables

- Segment scorecard with integrator lock-in rated per segment
- Entry-segment rationale, with deferred and parked segments and why
- Personas for each decision role, with their objections
- Vendor-risk pack outline covering data access, offshore controls and subcontractors
- Entry plan with target list, message, channel mix and next-segment gate

#### 05 · Outcome

- **Regional banks** chosen as the entry segment, with the reasons written down
- **Large banks** deferred, so sellers stop chasing integrator-held panels
- **Offshore-delivery questions** answered early, before a risk review stalls the deal
- **Expansion** decided at an agreed gate, not by whichever enquiry arrives

Sellers now open regional bank conversations on core modernisation and invite the third-party risk lead in early.

#### 06 · What changed in the client's GTM motion

Leadership now reviews US expansion quarterly at a segment gate, using the scorecard. A new segment opens only when it outscores the current one, and sellers add each new question from a bank's risk review to the vendor-risk pack.

### Repositioning a process-control maker on retrofit fit and lifecycle cost

**Service:** Repositioning · **Industry:** Industrial Technology · **Family:** GTM Strategy & Market Intelligence

*Plant buyers stopped shortlisting on controller specifications and started asking what a retrofit would disturb. A proof audit showed which claims the company could back, and the position was built on those.*

- **The challenge:** A specification-led story while plant buyers shortlist on retrofit fit and lifecycle cost.
- **Our approach:** Buyer research, a competitive review and a proof audit before choosing the position.
- **The result:** Sellers lead with retrofit fit and lifecycle cost, backed by reference plants.

| At a glance | |
|---|---|
| Client profile | Process-control hardware maker serving water, chemicals and food plants |
| Industry | Industrial Technology |
| Markets | UK and continental Europe |
| Services | Repositioning, Market Intelligence & Assessment, Sales Deck / Revenue Narrative |
| Engagement | Positioning reset |

#### 01 · Situation

Process-control makers often still sell on controller performance: speed, capacity and the newest platform. Their buyers have moved. Plants running ageing control systems want to modernise without rewiring the field or retraining operators, inside a planned shutdown, with spares and support costs known for the life of the asset. Competitors offering migration kits for the incumbent system win shortlists the company never sees, and sellers put the losses down to price.

#### 02 · Question

What do plant buyers now shortlist on, which of those claims can the company prove, and what position follows?

#### 03 · What LeadStrategus did

- **Research how plants now buy.** Interview control and instrumentation engineers, maintenance heads and operations directors at water, chemicals and food plants on how they shortlist, what a retrofit must leave untouched and how they weigh lifecycle cost.
- **Map the competitive frame.** Review how incumbent control vendors and integrators sell migration: wiring-compatible replacement kits, tools that convert existing control logic, and long-term support terms. Mark where none is credible.
- **Audit the proof.** Run win/loss interviews on recent deals, list reference sites willing to host visits, and pull installed-base field data on cutover times and stoppages after migration. Keep only claims the evidence supports.
- **Choose and state the position.** Test it with sellers and integrators, then write it plainly: the company modernises ageing plant controls inside a planned shutdown, on the wiring and operator screens the plant already has.

#### 04 · Evidence / deliverables

- Buyer research summary: shortlisting criteria by plant type and role
- Competitive map of migration offers and support terms
- Proof audit: win/loss findings, reference-site register and field performance summary
- Position statement and value proposition, with the evidence behind each claim
- Messaging framework for sellers and integrator partners

#### 05 · Outcome

- **Sellers** open with what the retrofit leaves untouched and how the cutover fits the shutdown
- **Reference plants** offered as site visits in live evaluations
- **Proposals** state spares and support terms alongside the purchase price
- **Unsupported claims** removed from datasheets and the website

Lost-deal reviews now check whether the buyer saw retrofit evidence and a reference plant before the shortlist closed.

#### 06 · What changed in the client's GTM motion

Product marketing keeps a proof register listing each claim, its evidence and the reference site behind it. New claims enter sales material only when the register shows proof, and quarterly win/loss interviews test whether the position still matches how plants shortlist.

- **Our approach:** Buyer interviews, a competitive frame and win/loss evidence before choosing the position.
- **The result:** A new target buyer and a position the company can prove.

| At a glance | |
|---|---|
| Client profile | Retail forecasting and replenishment software company |
| Industry | Retail Analytics |
| Markets | India, grocery and multi-format retail |
| Services | Repositioning, Market Intelligence & Assessment, Sales Deck / Revenue Narrative |
| Engagement | Research-led positioning reset |

#### 01 · Situation

Retail analytics companies in India often built their story when retailers had little analytics of their own. Now ERP vendors sell analytics alongside their retail suites, BI dashboards sit at head office, and larger chains run data teams. Store stock also fills online and quick-delivery orders, so replenishment has become the pressing problem. A pitch about dashboards and forecast accuracy stalls after the demo, and the company keeps cutting price to compensate.

#### 02 · Question

What do Indian retail buyers now need that ERP analytics, BI tools and data teams do not provide, and who owns that need?

#### 03 · What LeadStrategus did

- **Research how priorities shifted.** Interview heads of supply chain, category and data at grocery and multi-format retailers. A recurring theme: store replenishment must now cover online orders picked from the same shelves.
- **Map the competitive frame.** Compare how ERP-native analytics, BI tools and in-house data teams are bought and where each stops. ERP analytics report history; in-house teams build forecasts that rarely reach a store order.
- **Audit what the company can prove.** Review recent wins and losses. Losses cluster where the pitch claims better forecasts than the retailer's data team; wins come where the product turns forecasts into store orders.
- **Choose the buyer and the position.** Test options with buyers, name heads of supply chain as the target, and state the position plainly: the company turns a retailer's own forecasts into daily replenishment orders for every store and dark store, alongside the ERP and data team.

#### 04 · Evidence / deliverables

- Buyer research on shifting priorities, by retail format
- Competitive frame: ERP-native analytics, BI tools and in-house data teams
- Win/loss and proof audit of recent deals
- Target buyer profile for heads of supply chain
- Positioning statement and messaging brief for sales and marketing

#### 05 · Outcome

- **Sellers** opening with the replenishment problem heads of supply chain own
- **Retailers' data teams** brought into evaluations as allies
- **Forecast-accuracy claims** removed from the pitch and website
- **Leadership** qualifying out deals where the buyer only wants more reports

The company now leads with store and dark-store replenishment for heads of supply chain, and treats a request for more dashboards as a poor-fit signal.

#### 06 · What changed in the client's GTM motion

The positioning statement and competitive frame sit in a brief that product, marketing and sales sign off before any new deck, campaign or launch. Sellers tag wins and losses against the frame, so leadership sees when ERP analytics or in-house teams start winning again.

| At a glance | |
|---|---|
| Client profile | Global cloud provider |
| Industry | Cloud & Infrastructure |
| Markets | A Southeast Asian market |
| Services | Market Intelligence & Assessment, Research & Forecasting, OSINT |
| Engagement | Market assessment |

#### 01 · Situation

Global cloud providers growing in a Southeast Asian market often have no view of which companies drive cloud spend or which provider holds them. Banks, telcos, digital platforms and government-linked groups carry much of the demand, but their provider choices appear in no report. Sales covers accounts by size and sector, so a bank committed to a rival's local region gets the same effort as a platform still choosing. Territory plans and partner investment then follow company size instead of contestable spend.

#### 02 · Question

Who are the most important cloud buyers in the market, what is each provider's real share of their spend, and where should sales focus first?

#### 03 · What LeadStrategus did

- **Build the market from the bottom up.** Identify and profile the companies that drive cloud demand in the country, rather than starting from top-down totals.
- **Triangulate every estimate.** Infer each company's provider usage from several independent signals (public technology footprints, hiring, partner and case-study references, analyst data), then have an analyst review conflicts before anything enters the model.
- **Estimate share company by company.** Roll the validated company views up into a provider share estimate, with a confidence level shown for each figure.
- **Turn the model into a target list.** Show where the client is strong, where competitors hold accounts, and which accounts sales should prioritise.

#### 04 · Evidence / deliverables

- Company-level profiles of the top cloud-buying companies
- Bottom-up national market model with confidence levels
- Provider share estimate
- Tiered target-account list with the competitive position of each account

- **Top cloud buyers** profiled company by company, with the provider behind each
- **Share** estimated from cross-checked, company-level evidence
- **Sales coverage** moved towards accounts still split between providers
- **Account plans** for rival-held accounts now start from how firmly the rival holds them

Territory reviews now begin with named companies and the provider behind each, and leadership asks how confident an account figure is before moving sellers onto it.

#### 06 · What changed in the client's GTM motion

Planning moves from top-down market estimates to an account-level model. Each territory planning round now starts from that model: analysts refresh an account's provider view when new hiring, case studies or partner references appear, and sales tiers are read from it instead of being rebuilt every round.

### Finding where managed identity services are under-served in the US and India

**Service:** Market Intelligence & Assessment · **Industry:** Identity & Security · **Family:** GTM Strategy & Market Intelligence

*Cybersecurity is too wide a category to plan against. A clear boundary, maturity segments and a provider landscape show where managed identity demand outruns supply in each country.*

- **The challenge:** A wide security category with no boundary, segments or provider view for managed IAM.
- **Our approach:** Market boundary, maturity segments, demand drivers and a provider landscape per country.
- **The result:** A market view showing where each country's demand is under-served, and why.

| At a glance | |
|---|---|
| Client profile | Managed IAM services provider |
| Industry | Identity & Security |
| Markets | United States, India |
| Services | Market Intelligence & Assessment, OSINT / Market & Account Intelligence, GTM Setup |
| Engagement | Market and whitespace assessment |

#### 01 · Situation

Managed IAM providers growing across the US and India often plan against "cybersecurity", a category so wide it hides their own market. Most identity spend buys licences that clients' own teams run, which a managed provider does not win. Without a boundary, segments or a view of who serves each country, nobody can say where demand is unmet. Hiring and partner bets follow inbound enquiries, often into segments large integrators already hold.

#### 02 · Question

Where in the US and India does managed identity demand outrun current supply, and what drives it?

#### 03 · What LeadStrategus did

- **Draw the market boundary.** Include managed workforce IAM, IGA and PAM, where an outside provider runs access reviews, joiner-mover-leaver processes and privileged access. Exclude licence-only purchases and customer identity.
- **Segment by identity maturity.** Place organisations by how they run identity today: manual processes and spreadsheet access reviews, tools licensed but under-staffed, or a mature in-house identity team. The under-staffed middle is where managed demand concentrates.
- **Trace demand drivers in each country.** In the US, sector rules such as New York's DFS cybersecurity regulation, HIPAA access controls and SOX access reviews make firms evidence access control. In India, DPDP Act obligations, RBI's IT governance directions and SEBI's cyber resilience framework reach banks, NBFCs, brokers and asset managers.
- **Map providers and whitespace.** Profile global integrators, IT services majors, managed security providers and vendors' services arms in each country, marking where each is thin. Test the picture in practitioner interviews.

#### 04 · Evidence / deliverables

- Market definition with inclusions and exclusions
- Identity-maturity segmentation, with the public signals used for placement
- Demand-driver brief per country and regulated sector
- Provider landscape per country, with each provider type's strengths and gaps
- Whitespace map of segments where demand outruns current supply

#### 05 · Outcome

- **Licence-only demand** removed from the plan, leaving buyers who want identity run for them
- **Under-staffed, tool-owning firms** chosen as the core segment in both countries
- **Country priorities** set: mid-sized US banks, insurers and health systems below integrators' deal size; Indian NBFCs and brokers without identity teams
- **Hiring and partner plans** tied to the whitespace map instead of inbound enquiries

Leadership now invests by segment and country, and account list-building starts only once a segment is chosen.

#### 06 · What changed in the client's GTM motion

Planning now starts from a written market definition and maturity segmentation. When an obligation lands, such as the next phase of DPDP rules, the team updates the demand-driver brief and re-checks segment priorities.

- **The challenge:** Practice investment decided on partner conviction, with no evidence of what CHROs will fund.

| At a glance | |
|---|---|
| Client profile | Global advisory firm planning people-practice investment |
| Industry | Professional Services |
| Markets | Global; large and mid-sized employers |
| Services | Research & Forecasting, Market Intelligence & Assessment, Scenario Planning |
| Engagement | Forecasting programme |

Advisory firms' people practices often plan investment from partner conviction and last year's sales. Hire ahead of the wrong demand and consultants sit on the bench; hire late and competitors win the early engagements.

Which HR changes will CHROs fund, in which sectors and company sizes, and where should the practice invest ahead of demand?

#### 05 · Outcome

- **Practice leaders** set hiring plans from the funding outlook, segment by segment
- **Rejected hypotheses** dropped before they attract headcount
- **Sector leads** focus partner time where CHROs report approved budget
- **Watch signals** agreed for refreshing the outlook between surveys

Before approving a new hire, the investment committee now asks which segment's funding evidence supports it.

#### 06 · What changed in the client's GTM motion

The annual planning round now opens with the funding outlook, and each proposition lead must tie a growth case to a segment in it. Watch signals are refreshed every quarter, so the forecast is updated, not re-commissioned.

| At a glance | |
|---|---|

#### 05 · Outcome

- **Share range** with the evidence behind each bound
- **Assumptions** visible, sourced and open to challenge

Planning discussions move from arguing over one number to agreeing which assumptions to watch and what would change the plan.

Market sizing becomes a living model rather than a one-off report. When new data arrives, the team updates the affected assumption and sees how the range shifts, so budgets and targets follow the evidence.

### Setting clear commit, pause and pivot triggers for an industrial company's new markets
